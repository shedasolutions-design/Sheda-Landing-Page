The platform

Everything a property deal needs, in one place

Property is slow, paper heavy and built on trusting people you have never met. A deposit sits with someone you hope will return it. A title is a document that could be reprinted. An agent stands between the two people doing the deal and is paid for being there.

If you are buying or renting

  • Search verified listings and filter by what matters to you
  • Book an inspection directly with the owner, around their real availability
  • Make an offer, negotiate, and agree terms in the app
  • Pay into escrow that a contract releases, not a person
  • Receive the documents on chain, where they cannot be quietly reissued

If you are selling or letting

  • List a property and have it minted as an NFT you own
  • Receive and review offers from verified buyers
  • Release documents and take payment when the conditions are met
  • Manage tenants, leases, renewals and deposits from one place
  • Raise a dispute or a damage claim, with an on-chain record

The blockchain is deliberately not the product. It is what makes the escrow releasable by rule rather than by permission, and the documents impossible to quietly change.

Fractional ownership · now live on testnet

Own part of one specific flat, and earn its rent

Property is the asset most people would choose and the one most are priced out of. The usual answer is a fund: you own a slice of a pool, you do not know which building, and you trust an intermediary to tell you what it earned.

Sheda Collections does the opposite. You buy shares in one specific flat, often before it is built. The rent that flat earns is divided among the people holding its shares, in proportion, on chain. Nobody reports the figure to you, because nobody has to.

NEP-171 NFT The flat Unit 1B. A specific address, a specific tenant, a specific rent.
Owns it The vault An account holds the NFT, so the vault is the landlord.
NEP-141 token The shares Identical claims that divide, add together and sell in part.

A flat cannot be divided without destroying what makes it an asset, so it stays a single NFT. The claims on its rent are a fungible token, because ten people splitting a flat do not own ten different things. A tenth of the rent is a tenth of the rent.

How a raise works

Money moves a phase at a time, and never on one person's word

01

A builder opens a raise

With the flats it divides into and a schedule that must release exactly all of it. Their record and the stake behind their promises are public.

02

People commit to a flat

Choosing the specific unit they want a share of. The money goes to escrow, not to the builder.

03

An independent manager inspects

The builder says a phase is finished. Somebody who is not the builder has to agree before anything is paid.

04

Holders get seven days to object

A quarter of them objecting holds the payment. A vote nobody can exercise is not a safeguard.

05

If the build stalls, the stake pays

Builders post their own money against their promises. A guarantee you can walk away from is not a guarantee.

06

Once let, the rent divides itself

It arrives at the flat's vault and is split between shareholders in proportion, without anyone pressing a button.

Builders and managers are ranked on what the chain recorded them doing, and every profile shows its working. A builder with too little history reads “not rated yet” rather than being given a number nobody can account for.
