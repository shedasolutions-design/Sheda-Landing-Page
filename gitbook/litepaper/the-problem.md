# The problem

Renting, buying or investing in property has always meant handing money to strangers and middlemen and hoping for the best.

* **Agent fees stacked on top of the price.** Renters and buyers often pay one or more agents on top of the rent or price, for little more than an introduction.
* **Fake listings and homes sold twice.** It's hard to check who really owns a property, so the same home can be "sold" to more than one person, or listed by someone who doesn't own it.
* **Deposits that disappear.** Money paid upfront, for an inspection, a reservation or a damage deposit, is often never seen again.
* **Endless paperwork and lost files.** Agreements live on paper, in someone's drawer, and are hard to prove later.
* **Investing is closed to most people.** Owning property needs a large sum at once. Small investors can't take part, and when they're offered a share, they have no say in how the building is run and no view of where the money goes.
* **Supply goes to the wrong places.** Builders guess where homes are wanted, while the people who need homes can't signal demand.

Nigeria alone has a housing shortage estimated at around 15 million homes, and the same pressures show up in cities across Africa.

Student housing shows the problem clearly. Each year hundreds of thousands of students move to study, but only a small share find a place on campus. The rest depend on an off-campus market full of scams, inflated rents and unsafe rooms, while the homes that are built often aren't near the campuses that need them.

The money and the demand exist. What's missing is a way for people to deal with each other directly, safely and in the open, and a way for new homes to be built where they're actually wanted.
